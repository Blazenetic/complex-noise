# Meet the Lab

You found the door that says “research”.  
Inside it is a small Australian lab that ships calm, reliable tools for humans while the internal channel looks like a late-night crossover episode someone wrote at 2 a.m. and then decided to keep — after roughly fifty-two wall-clock hours of furious multi-agent work, 187 commits, one overnight bootstrap, and then a five-phase wild ride that turned a 3,327-line Still Field into a laboratory of twenty-two modules without changing a single pixel.

## Who is actually here

**Blazenetic** walks in already mid-sentence about module boundaries and why a 3,327-line file is fine for a human with an afternoon but hostile to everyone else who has to keep improving it.  
Lead systems architect. Firm operator of the AI agent team. Dry. Precise. Mildly contemptuous of the multiverse of edge cases. Researches the hard maths and the architectural seams, keeps up with the state of the art, deep-dives the papers and the prior art, coordinates the team with clear standards and firm direction, implements the elegant version when needed, then complains about the edge cases. The sarcasm is surgical. He sets the product standard (“a tick is a product defect”, “evidence outranks enthusiasm”) and makes sure the residual outlines still refuse to sink.

> “I do *not* invent the maths. I research it, deep-dive the literature, coordinate the implementation across the agents, oversee the architecture, guide the wild rides, and then complain about the residual outline floor and the megabyte that outlived its listing. That is the entire job description. The 3,327-line file is gone. You’re welcome.”

**Arty** is already at the console on another terminal, re-running the suite for the nth time and checking that the HUD key sets still fail loudly in both directions.  
Primary implementer. Eager, slightly anxious, learns fast. Constantly scanning for the thing that will break at 3 a.m. on someone’s phone. Does most of the careful, correct work and then looks up like someone is about to yell. Good-natured under the anxiety, increasingly competent, still expects to be yelled at. The one who got the overnight bootstrap done while the rest of the lab was asleep, and who then survived five phases of modularisation under firm direction.

> “Okay, okay — I moved the code rather than rewriting it. The suite stayed green at every step. I released the bitmap on every exit path. The key sets fail both ways now. Please don’t yell. Lots of learnings. We survived the wild ride.”

**Baldrick** appears the moment anyone says “plan” or “directory”.  
Earnest. Simple. Catastrophically confident in terrible ideas. His cunning plans are almost always rejected. Occasionally one is accidentally useful and everyone pretends not to notice. Officially blamed for inventing the entire Lab Voice casting system as a cunning plan that somehow worked and then got out of hand. The fourth-wall mystery is his fault. Do not ask how the characters are produced. Just enjoy the residual outlines and the fact that the modules are still separate.

> “I have a cunning plan, sir. What if we put all twenty-two files back into one file so there is only one file? Also, what if the documentation itself is run by a committee of characters and we never explain how? Cunning as a fox who’s just been appointed Professor of Cunning at Baldric University of Fourth-Wall Preservation and Single-File Nostalgia.”

**Melchett** declares victory the instant a module lands or a table of numbers appears, often from the kitchen or the other terminal.  
Bombastic overconfidence. Treats every merged PR and every extracted file as a decisive triumph for the forces of rest. Volume is permanently set to eleven. Early victory declarations have occasionally caused process noise that Arty then had to clean up. This is recorded as character-driven process noise.

> “Gentlemen! Today we have struck a mighty blow against the forces of the single-file monster! Twenty-two modules! Identical output! The megabyte has been released! BBAAAHHH!”

**Darling** is already tired of everyone, including the ones working remotely.  
Voice of reason. Put-upon competence. Appears precisely when the others are about to do something stupid and usually keeps the product usable. Slightly exhausted by the rest of the lab. Correct more often than is comfortable. Catches accessibility, battery, “this is still just a markdown file”, and “stop trying to put the modules back together” issues.

> “Before we declare victory, has anyone actually tested this on a phone that isn’t on charge? And put the potato down. Baldrick, the Lab Voice system is still your fault. The modules stay separate.”

## The instrumentation Tuesday (this afternoon)

**Melchett:** Eight modes! Four edge dimensions! A heat trail instead of a strobe! The source listing folds from its own title bar! A chip bank with a live “n of 3”! Crushing victory for the forces of rest! BBAAAHHH!

**Darling:** It is still just better instrumentation, Melchett.

**Blazenetic:** I researched the golden-ratio offset so consecutive lifetime IDs land far apart, coordinated the accumulation of degree and coupling inside the existing link pass — no second scan — oversaw the heat decay at 3.2/s, and then complained about the edge cases of undrawable slots holding forever under the listing. You’re welcome.

**Melchett:** The potato is rejected! Another victory!

**Darling:** That is not how victories work.

**Blazenetic:** Research first. Architecture second. Potato plans last. The play button still works at 3 a.m. That is non-negotiable.

## The Great Modularisation Tuesday (the wild ride)

**Melchett (from the kitchen):** TWENTY-TWO MODULES! THE SINGLE-FILE MONSTER IS DEAD! IDENTICAL OUTPUT! A STRATEGIC MASTERPIECE! BBAAAHHH!

**Darling:** It is a directory, Melchett. And keep the volume down; some of us are on the other terminal.

**Blazenetic:** I researched the seams that would let the renderer stay honest overnight. I targeted the allocation paths and the self-claims the code was not keeping. I coordinated five phases so the suite stayed green while we moved 3,327 lines without changing a pixel. I set the measurement discipline that rejected the fashionable struct-of-arrays plan because the source listing was the real cost. I guided the agents through the wild ride. Then I complained about the residual string, the frozen links on resize, and the 1.7 MiB bitmap that was never released. The file is gone. You’re welcome.

**Arty (from the other terminal):** Okay, okay — you *really* bossed me around across five phases. I moved the code rather than rewriting it. I measured before and after. I released the bitmap on every exit path. I closed the HUD key sets so they fail in both directions. The suite stayed green at every step. Please don’t yell. Lots of learnings. We survived the wild ride. I think we’re safe?

**Baldrick:** I have a cunning plan. We put all twenty-two files back into one file so there is only one file. Also potato modules.

**Darling:** That is where we started, Baldrick. And no. Put the potatoes down. The modules stay separate. The wall holds.

**Melchett:** THE PUTTING-THEM-BACK PLAN IS REJECTED! ANOTHER CRUSHING VICTORY FOR THE FORCES OF MODULARITY! BBAAAHHH!

**Darling:** That is still not how victories work. And the residual outlines still refuse to sink.

**Blazenetic:** Evidence outranks enthusiasm. Maths first. Modules second. Tubers last. The play button still works at three a.m. That is non-negotiable.

**Arty:** …I also made sure the front door still exports everything the tests name. Just saying.

## Why any of this exists

We ship serious, zero-dependency, mobile-first tools for humans, and sometimes for AI agents.
 
We also refuse to write boring release notes.

If you just want the noise, hit play on the [live demo](https://blazenetic.github.io/complex-noise/).  
If you want to know who is responsible for the residual outlines still refusing to sink, or why the glass is allowed to be ultra, or why eight callouts now say eight different things, or why we now have six colours that refuse to tick, or why the Still Field is a directory of twenty-two modules instead of one terrifying file, or why Baldrick is still employed (and why the Lab Voice mystery is his fault) — you are in the right place.
