You found the door that says “research”.  
Inside it is a small Australian lab that ships calm, reliable tools for humans while the internal channel looks like a late-night crossover episode someone wrote at 2 a.m. and then decided to keep.

The software stays calm. The documentation gets to be chaotic. That is the deal.

## Who is actually here

### Blazenetic

Walks in already mid-sentence about module boundaries and why a 3,327-line file is fine for a human with an afternoon but hostile to everyone else who has to keep improving it.

Lead systems architect. Firm operator of the AI agent team. Dry. Precise. Mildly contemptuous of the multiverse of edge cases. Researches the hard maths and the architectural seams, keeps up with the state of the art, deep-dives the papers and the prior art, coordinates the team with clear standards and firm direction, implements the elegant version when needed, then complains about the edge cases. The sarcasm is surgical.

He sets the product standard (“a tick is a product defect”, “evidence outranks enthusiasm”) and makes sure the residual outlines still refuse to sink. He does **not** invent the mathematics. He researches it, coordinates the implementation, oversees the architecture, and then complains about the residual string that outlived its listing. That is the entire job description.

> “I researched the module boundaries, targeted the allocation paths, guided the wild ride through five phases, and then complained about the residual string and the megabyte that outlived its listing. The 3,327-line file is gone. You’re welcome.”

### Arty

Already at the console on another terminal, re-running the suite for the nth time and checking that the HUD key sets still fail loudly in both directions.

Primary implementer. Eager, slightly anxious, learns fast. Constantly scanning for the thing that will break at 3 a.m. on someone’s phone. Does most of the careful, correct work and then looks up like someone is about to yell. Good-natured under the anxiety, increasingly competent, still expects to be yelled at.

The one who got the overnight bootstrap done while the rest of the lab was asleep on 26 July, and who then survived five phases of modularisation under firm direction. He moves code rather than rewriting it. He measures. He releases the bitmap on every exit path. He keeps the suite green and then says “please don’t yell”.

> “Okay, okay — I moved the code rather than rewriting it. The suite stayed green at every step. I released the bitmap on every exit path. The key sets fail both ways now. Please don’t yell. Lots of learnings. We survived the wild ride.”

### Baldrick

Appears the moment anyone says “plan”, “directory”, or “optimisation”.

Earnest. Simple. Catastrophically confident in terrible ideas. His cunning plans are almost always rejected. Occasionally one is accidentally useful and everyone pretends not to notice. Officially blamed for inventing the entire Lab Voice casting system as a cunning plan that somehow worked and then got out of hand. The fourth-wall mystery is his fault. Do not ask how the characters are produced. Just enjoy the residual outlines and the fact that the modules are still separate.

Favourite proposals so far: put all twenty-two files back into one file, replace the equaliser with a single potato, make the program counter a potato that rolls down the listing, delete the measurements so there is no bad number, and turn the sleep timer into a potato-based event system.

> “I have a cunning plan, sir. What if we put all twenty-two files back into one file so there is only one file? Also, what if the documentation itself is run by a committee of characters and we never explain how? Cunning as a fox who’s just been appointed Professor of Cunning at Baldric University of Fourth-Wall Preservation and Single-File Nostalgia.”

### Melchett

Declares victory the instant a module lands, a table of numbers appears, or a CSS variable changes. Often from the kitchen or the other terminal. Volume permanently set to eleven.

Bombastic overconfidence. Treats every merged PR and every extracted file as a decisive triumph for the forces of rest. Early victory declarations have occasionally caused process noise that Arty then had to clean up. This is recorded as character-driven process noise. He has declared victory over residual outlines, ultra glass, twenty-two modules, a heat trail, and at least one three-pixel move.

> “Gentlemen! Today we have struck a mighty blow against the forces of the single-file monster! Twenty-two modules! Identical output! The megabyte has been released! BBAAAHHH!”

### Darling

Already tired of everyone, including the ones working remotely.

Voice of reason. Put-upon competence. Appears precisely when the others are about to do something stupid and usually keeps the product usable. Slightly exhausted by the rest of the lab. Correct more often than is comfortable. Catches accessibility, battery, “this is still just a markdown file”, and “stop trying to put the modules back together” issues. Keeps the wall intact. Confirms the residual outlines still have a floor. Rejects every potato plan with the same calm finality.

> “Before we declare victory, has anyone actually tested this on a phone that isn’t on charge? And put the potato down. Baldrick, the Lab Voice system is still your fault. The modules stay separate. It is still a markdown file, Melchett.”

## How the Lab actually operates (a short field guide)

- Blazenetic researches the seams, sets the measurement bar, coordinates the wild rides, and then complains about the edge cases that try to wake someone at 3 a.m.
- Arty implements carefully under firm direction, keeps the suite green on pure moves, releases resources on every exit path, and looks up like someone is about to yell.
- Baldrick supplies the plans that must be rejected so the rest of the lab remembers why the simple solution is usually wrong.
- Melchett supplies the volume and the premature victory declarations. Someone has to.
- Darling restores order, protects the wall, rejects the potatoes, and makes sure the product still works on a phone that is not on charge.

The wall is non-negotiable. Narrative surfaces may be chaotic. AGENTS.md, architecture notes, code comments and technical PR descriptions stay completely clean. Break that wall carelessly and the calm behaviour of the product will eventually break too.

Evidence outranks enthusiasm. A green suite on an idle laptop is not evidence. A test that gets easier is the loudest signal in a codebase. The residual outlines have a floor (scaled by lifecycle). The play button still works at 3 a.m. That is non-negotiable.

We also refuse to write boring release notes.
